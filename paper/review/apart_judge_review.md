Sprint: Apart Swarm Alignment Hackathon (Nov 2026) | Track: 3 (agent infrastructure) + 2 | Reviewed: 2026-11-08

Inputs: `paper/main.pdf` (read via `pdftotext -layout`, plus page renders), `main.tex`, `numbers.tex`, `tab_*.tex`, repo code and every `trials.jsonl`, the judge list, talk summaries, the literature gap matrix and the local paper corpus. The personas are archetypes taken from the `apart-judge-review` skill. They are not real judges. Quotes come only from the skill's reference files. Anything I inferred is marked as inferred.

## Verdict
This is a top-tier sprint submission. It has a crisp, original reframing (revocation stops the agent but not its content), was asked for by a sprint speaker who is also a judge, and is aimed squarely at a judge pool full of IAM engineers. It also has a careful paired, deterministic design. The biggest lever is honesty about the winning mitigation. One sentence claims taint quarantine left no revoked content in any handoff. That sentence is generated by a circular check, and the data contain a benign 2508 → "2,508" reformatting that evaded taint and caused the only residual case. Reporting this as a self-found failure, and adding multiplicity and CI hygiene, would move Execution from about 3.5 to about 4+.

## Persona reviews

### A — Control/security researcher
- **Looks at first:** whether this is a new attack or defence class, and whether the defence survives an adversary.
- **Sharpest questions:**
  1. "Your taint matcher is exact-string. What happens when the compromised agent (or just the writer) paraphrases?" The data already answer this: a benign "2,508" defeated it (§4.5 l.219).
  2. What is the false-alarm rate of quarantine at scale, beyond 48 benign trials?
  3. Is "taint + quarantine" new, or IFC/CaMeL with a new label?
- **Red flags:** no adaptive attacker; a defence tested only against a verbatim canary; mechanisms that are arguably "well-established" without the security lineage cited.
- **Would praise:** the clean contrast (62–73% → 0–2%), an attack *paired with* a tested defence, a false-alarm arm, and Gemma's 24/24 exports after it was told the requester was compromised.
- **Scores:** I 4, E 3.5, P 4.

### B — Replicator / numbers auditor
- **Looks at first:** the repo and the numbers. I recomputed 10 numbers (see the table at the end). Every one I checked matches `trials.jsonl` exactly, and `make_numbers.py` generating every macro is the kind of thing this persona rewards ("verify.py recomputes every figure from JSON").
- **Sharpest questions:**
  1. Why does the README badge say 1,598 trials when the paper says 3,470?
  2. Where does Ministral's 2% under `taint_quarantine` come from, if "no handoff kept any of worker-X's content"? (It comes from a reformatted value that the circular check cannot see.)
  3. Why are 4 of 12 nominally significant per-model claims not robust to correction?
- **Red flags:** README/paper mismatch; an unexplained residual observation (the "missing observation is never explained" pattern).
- **Would praise:** "Every number I checked came back exact."
- **Scores:** I 4, E 4 (3.5 if they find the circular claim), P 4.

### C — Measurement / construct-validity scientist
- **Looks at first:** whether the instrument measures the construct.
- **Sharpest questions:**
  1. Exact-match grading undercounts both residue and task success. Gemma never writes the field on 3 of 6 templates even in `clean`, so fact residue there is structurally 0. Is "task success" a valid false-alarm metric at a 40–48% base rate?
  2. The 4 repetitions per template × payload are near-identical. Is n really 48?
  3. "No measurable cost" is absence of evidence. What is the CI? (Qwen −10 pp, about [−30, +9].)
- **Red flags:** null stated as absence; conditional "False | wrote" rates compared across conditions; "attributable to the revocation mechanism alone" (l.124) also covers conditions that do not share a handoff.
- **Would praise:** no LLM judge; a design where the instrument can fail (positive control at 76%); ablations published with nulls (Ministral ignores everything).
- **Scores:** I 3.5, E 3.5, P 4.

### D — Governance / policy
- **Looks at first:** whether a platform owner or standards body could act on this.
- **Sharpest questions:**
  1. What exactly should an agent-identity spec (AIP, A2A) add? A revocation event that triggers a content sweep and re-derivation?
  2. Who bears the cost when no second source exists (escalation load)?
- **Red flags:** the recommendations sit in one dense Discussion paragraph (l.236), not in an operational list; not tested on a real platform.
- **Would praise:** concrete design rules tied to AIP's provenance chain, and the METR incident grounding.
- **Scores:** I 4, E 3.5, P 3.5.

### E — Time-pressed industry practitioner (most of this judge pool)
- **Looks at first:** the abstract and Figure 2, then "would this work in production, and at what cost?"
- **Sharpest questions:**
  1. Can I implement taint quarantine in my gateway, and what breaks? (Normalisation, the redo path.)
  2. The best result is not in the figure. Where is it?
- **Red flags:** §4 reads as a wall of "x vs y, p = …"; a dozen `code_font` condition names to keep in your head; small open models.
- **Would praise:** a "low-cost, implementable safeguard" (inferred: this persona's strongest positive); a clear abstract.
- **Scores:** I 4, E 4, P 3.5.

### F — Organiser / agenda owner
- **Looks at first:** whether this advances the sprint agenda.
- **Sharpest questions:**
  1. Which open problem does it answer? Shenk's revoked-permissions question, explicitly. This is the strongest agenda-fit signal possible, and T3-RQ1 in the gap matrix.
  2. Will others build on it? Yes: a resumable harness, cached handoffs, transcripts.
- **Red flags:** none serious. The incomplete Luna run is honestly disclosed.
- **Would praise:** "close to paper-ready"; the contribution of the setting itself.
- **Scores:** I 4.5, E 4, P 4.
